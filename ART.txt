ARTICLE              INFO                              ABSTRACT

Keywords:                                              Scene Graph Generation (SGG) plays an important role in scene understanding because all of the objects
Scene graph generation                                 and relations in an image can be abstracted into a concise topological graph. Due to the complexity of
Transformer                                            visual scenes, including mutual occlusion between objects and semantic ambiguity, SGG is still a challenging
Attention redirection
                                                       task. Most of the existing models only focused on the context of a single object while contexts provided
Translation embedding
                                                       by paired objects are ignored. In this paper, we propose an Attention Redirection Transformer (ART) to
Scene understanding
                                                       extract pair-level contexts specifically, which is divided into an attention distraction stage and an attention
                                                       integration stage. In this way, the attention of the model is forced to be redirected, which explores the implicit
                                                       information in the background. In addition, to incorporate the semantic information of predicates, a Semantic
                                                       Oriented Learning Module (SOL) is designed, which may assist in getting better textual semantics and also
                                                       prompts cross-modal information fusion. At last, a self-diversity driven Dual Translation Embedding Module
                                                       (DTM) is designed, which refines representations of subject and object and makes them distinct. Experimental
                                                       results on the Visual Genome dataset demonstrate the effectiveness of our proposed method. Moreover, our
                                                       method outperforms state-of-the-art methods on the mR@K metric. The source codes are released on Github:
                                                       https://github.com/Nora-Zhang98/ART-SOL.

Semantic embedding of predicates
    In general, the semantic embeddings of predicate classes are all
obtained from the pre-trained Glove [13] offline. They are more stable
and less likely to be affected by data noises compared with the learned
representations. After observing all predicates, we note that they can
be divided into three types:
    (1) One word. More than half of the predicates only contain one
word, such as ‘‘holding’’, ‘‘under’’, ‘‘watching’’, etc. For these predi-
cates, the predicate semantic embeddings are directly obtained from
the word embeddings of the corresponding labels.
    (2) Two words. For those predicates which are composed of more
than one word, they are split into single words first. Meanwhile, we find
a phenomenon that for the two-word predicates, it is usually the first
word that takes on more semantic information, while the next word
is usually a preposition without much sense, such as ‘‘attached to’’,
‘‘looking at’’, etc. Based on this observation, as shown in Algorithm
1, the semantic embeddings of each word in a predicate are fused.
An extra weight coefficient 𝛼 is introduced to make the model focus
more on the semantically rich word. For two-word predicates, 𝛼 is the
coefficient of the former word, and (1 − 𝛼) is for the latter.
    (3) Three words. As for three-word predicates, the semantic focus is
often on the middle word such as ‘‘in front of’’. So for these predicates,
𝛼 is assigned to the middle word while other words are assigned the
